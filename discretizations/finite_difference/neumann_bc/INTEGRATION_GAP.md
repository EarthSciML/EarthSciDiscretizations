## The precise gap

`build_ode_problem` (the Layer-C numeric path) does **not** lower boundary
conditions through the declarative rule engine. It lowers them through an
**imperative shadow** in ESS `discretize.jl`:

- `_arrayop_lift_equations!` → `_apply_nonperiodic_bcs!`
  (`packages/EarthSciSerialization.jl/src/discretize.jl:355`, defined at `:767`)
  reads the ESM `boundary_conditions` map directly and substitutes ghost reads
  per kind.
- `_check_bc_supported` (`discretize.jl:746`) hard-codes the supported set:
  `dirichlet` and **zero-flux** neumann only. For a neumann BC with a nonzero
  `value` it throws:

  ```
  RuleEngineError("E_BC_UNSUPPORTED",
      "nonzero-Neumann boundary condition on '<var>' along '<dim>' is not yet
       supported by the arrayop lift (requires grid-spacing-aware ghost
       extrapolation)")
  ```

So the declarative `neumann_bc` rule (`u_ghost = index(u, 1) + h·value` at xmin,
`index(u, N) + h·value` at xmax, with `h` bound by `bind_side_spacing`) is never
consulted by the integration path. The ghost math it expresses is exactly the
"grid-spacing-aware ghost extrapolation" the error message says is missing — but
wiring it into the solve requires ESS to route BC lowering through the rule
engine (the **makearray-bc-lowering** work) and then retire
`_apply_nonperiodic_bcs!`. Both are ESS-side, mayor-sequenced, and out of scope
for an ESD rule-authoring bead.

Closing this in ESD would mean hand-coding a nonzero-Neumann branch into the
imperative `_apply_nonperiodic_bcs!` / `_check_bc_supported` shadow — i.e.
exactly the imperative operator that DECLARATIVE-OR-FAIL forbids ("NO
imperative/hand-coded operator"). So we STOP and report.
